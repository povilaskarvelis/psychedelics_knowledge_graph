About

A living map of psychedelic research, built to keep scientific knowledge organized, connected, and easily explorable

Reasons

Psychedelic science is a broad and rapidly evolving field, spanning research from molecular mechanisms and brain function to phenomenology, clinical outcomes, public health, and real-world use across dozens of compounds. Its breadth and pace make it increasingly difficult to keep track of how the field is changing, where evidence is accumulating, how findings connect or diverge, and where gaps remain.

This challenge is not unique to psychedelic research. Scientific findings are still primarily stored across scattered narrative publications. Our current evidence synthesis methods, reviews and meta-analyses, bring evidence together around defined research questions, but they are limited in scope, require substantial expert labor and provide static snapshots that can become quickly outdated as new evidence emerges. Together, these forms of knowledge storage and synthesis make it difficult to navigate the constantly evolving research landscape.

Emerging AI tools make it possible to address both of these problems by streamlining literature discovery, screening, evidence extraction, and organization into a largely automated workflow that operates continuously and at scale. The structured knowledge produced through this workflow can form a knowledge graph that is updated as new evidence appears, audited and corrected, accessed by people and machines, and used to support new analyses and evidence syntheses.

Psychedelics Knowledge Graph applies this approach to psychedelic science. It converts findings from selected papers into structured knowledge that powers an interactive graph and dashboard. Researchers can move between field-level patterns and individual studies, filter the evidence around their interests, follow emerging trends, identify connections and gaps across the literature, and trace individual findings back to the papers from which they came. AI agents and analytic tools can access public graph data through the REST API or MCP server.

Research community feedback

This is a living research resource, and errors and omissions are expected as the literature grows and the evidence pipeline develops. The project is open source, making its methods and code available for inspection, reuse, and improvement. It is designed to evolve as researchers identify problems, point to missing evidence, and suggest clearer ways to represent the research.
