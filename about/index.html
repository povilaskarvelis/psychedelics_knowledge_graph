About

Why the Psychedelics Knowledge Graph exists and how it is intended to support a more accessible, auditable, and continuously updated view of psychedelic research.

Why this project

We are entering the era of agentic science, where AI agents can support many steps of knowledge generation. This creates an opportunity to address long-standing inefficiencies in academic workflows: findings are scattered across source reports, evidence is split across disciplinary silos, and reviews require substantial expert labor, become static snapshots once published, and are difficult to query or reuse. As a result, human researchers struggle to see relationships, trends, gaps, and the overall shape of a field, while AI agents cannot easily use the evidence directly.

Agentic tools make it possible to build a new type of living evidence system, where literature discovery, screening, evidence extraction, visualization, and updating are part of a continuous workflow. Findings can be converted into structured, provenance-rich evidence that remains linked to source reports and evidence locators, so the evidence base can be searched, corrected, reused, and extended as the literature changes.

The Psychedelics Knowledge Graph applies this model to psychedelic research, where a fast-growing literature spans clinical, biological, brain, behavioral, and translational work that is often read separately. Selected reports are converted into structured findings that power an interactive graph and dashboard. Human researchers can move from field-level patterns and gaps to source studies visually, while agents and analytic tools can query the same provenance-rich evidence directly.

Community feedback

This is a living research resource, and errors and omissions are expected as the literature grows and the evidence pipeline develops. The project is designed to improve over time as researchers and other informed readers identify problems, point to missing evidence, and suggest clearer ways to represent the research.
