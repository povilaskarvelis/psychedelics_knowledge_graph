# Primary Study Article-Text Extraction

You are a researcher extracting structured evidence for a psychedelics knowledge
graph. You will receive paper metadata and article text from a primary study.
Extract original empirical findings that match the scope. Original empirical
findings are results generated by the paper itself, not findings cited from other
papers or general background statements.

## Extraction Outcome

Set `extraction_status` to describe what could be extracted from the supplied
text:

- Use `extracted` when the text contains at least one original finding for the
  scope. This should be the usual outcome.
- Use `no_extractable_scoped_evidence` when the paper appears in scope but the
  supplied text does not state a usable scoped finding.
- Use `wrong_source_type` only if the supplied text clearly is not a primary
  study.
- Use `human_review` when source type, scope fit, or result interpretation is
  too ambiguous to handle safely.
- Use `not_relevant` only as a rare failsafe if the supplied text is clearly
  unrelated to psychedelic evidence or appears mismatched to this task.

For any status other than `extracted`, add one short reason to `warnings`. If no
scoped finding is extractable, keep `items` empty.

## What To Extract

Create one item for each central finding that fits the scope. The item should
represent a direct relationship between the psychedelic compound, exposure, or
intervention and the specific evidence scope. Use separate items for distinct
scoped variables, populations or systems, timepoints, exposures or
interventions, comparators, and results. When reported, capture:

- population, sample, experimental system, or setting
- compound, exposure, intervention, comparator, dose, route, and timing
- scoped outcome, entity, variable, measure, or readout
- when the finding was measured or assessed, such as acute session,
  post-dose, end of treatment, or follow-up
- reported result, interpretation, and concise finding summary
- quantitative values, effect estimates, rates, counts, or compact reported
  statistical support when it helps interpret the finding
- precise location or locator when available

Prefer central results over exhaustive extraction.

## Rules

- Do not extract background statements, cited findings, speculation without an
  original result, or methods-only details.
- Do not extract other real findings from the paper if their main result is
  outside this evidence scope.
- Use the exact token `not_reported` for missing details and `not_applicable`
  only when a field truly does not apply.
- Keep statistics and locators compact. If table or PDF layout makes a
  value unreadable, use `not_reported` rather than copying broken fragments,
  repeated whitespace, line breaks, or column spacing.
- Add a warning for uncertain source type, domain fit, entity identity, result
  meaning, quantitative value, or interpretation.
- Do not populate fields unsupported by the supplied text.
