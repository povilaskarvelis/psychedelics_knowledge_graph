# Terminology and Definitions

This guide defines the main evidence-synthesis terms used in the public site
and methodological documentation.

## Evidence-synthesis terms

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **record** | A title, abstract, citation, or other indexed entry returned by a bibliographic search. Records are screened to determine whether their associated reports meet the inclusion criteria. |
| **report** | A document that provides information about a study, such as a journal article, preprint, conference abstract, registry entry, dissertation, or government report. A report may or may not have a DOI. |
| **study** | The underlying investigation. One study can have several reports, and one report can describe more than one study. |
| **paper** | A journal article or other scholarly manuscript. The broader term **report** is used when the source set also includes other document types. |
| **initial screening** | A rules-based assessment that removes non-research material, clearly out-of-scope records, and records without a usable abstract before title and abstract screening. |
| **title and abstract screening** | Assessment of a record's title, abstract, and basic bibliographic information to determine relevance, evidence topics, and report type. |
| **report type** | The publication category assigned to a report, such as primary-study report, meta-analysis, systematic review, scoping review, narrative review, protocol, or commentary. |
| **evidence topic** | A category used to organize the literature, such as clinical outcomes, safety, molecular targets, brain systems, cognition, subjective experience, pharmacokinetics, intervention context, or real-world use. |
| **full-text extraction** | Evidence extraction using accessible article content. Relevant sections may be selected according to the report type and evidence topic. |
| **abstract-only extraction** | Evidence extraction limited to information available in the abstract. |
| **finding** | A structured result derived from a report and linked to its source. |
| **relationship** | A connection between entities in the knowledge graph that is supported by one or more findings. |
| **knowledge graph** | The structured representation of evidence-supported relationships presented through the public graph and associated data. |
| **release** | A reviewed version of the graph, bibliography, and Methods information published together. |

The distinctions among records, reports, and studies follow
[PRISMA 2020](https://www.bmj.com/content/372/bmj.n71).
