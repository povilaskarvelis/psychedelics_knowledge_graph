Focus on intervention-context evidence: psychotherapy, preparation,
integration, set and setting, facilitator or therapist role, music,
psychological support, manualized therapy, implementation, or
treatment-model findings.

Prioritize:

- intervention component or context feature
- population and clinical/nonclinical setting
- delivery format, session phase, comparator/context, and dose or session
  context when these are part of the finding
- relationship to outcomes, safety, adherence, or implementation

Normalize the visible graph anchor to a recognizable research topic when
possible, for example:

- preparation, integration, or combined preparation–integration protocols
- music, sensory environment, or set and setting
- facilitator role, therapeutic alliance, or psychological support
- group therapy, remote or at-home delivery, or residential/retreat delivery
- a recurring psychotherapy model such as CBT, mindfulness-based intervention,
  ACT, or motivational enhancement therapy
- ceremonial/ritual context, cultural adaptation, therapist training,
  implementation/feasibility, or access/equity

Preserve a named therapy, protocol, delivery model, or ceremonial model as the
specific evidence label. Normalize spelling and wording variants to the
researcher-facing topic used by the graph. Well-represented topics should keep
their own graph node; sparse one-off models or supports may use an explicit
long-tail bucket such as `Other psychotherapy models` or `Other session
supports`. Do not expose generic organizational buckets such as `Session
components`, `Therapeutic approaches`, or `Delivery formats` as graph nodes.

Capture therapy orientation, quantity/intensity, provider role, fidelity,
manualization, implementation, access, or training context only when it is a
central evidence point rather than generic trial background.

Do not extract ordinary trial methods, blinding, session timing/number,
follow-up schedule, or the mere
fact that a drug was administered unless context is a central evidence question.
Dose, route, infusion schedule, compound identity, outcome scales, acute
subjective effects, adverse events, and baseline demographic or personality
traits remain metadata or belong to their corresponding evidence domains; they
are not treatment/context nodes by themselves.

Do not create standalone clinical efficacy, safety, subjective-experience,
brain-system, cognitive/behavioral, molecular, or pharmacokinetic items here.
The intervention component or care context should remain the item anchor.
