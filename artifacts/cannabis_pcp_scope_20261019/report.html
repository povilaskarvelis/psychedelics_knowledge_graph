Keep PCP in the core; keep standalone cannabis outside it; fix PCP discovery coverage.

Assessment date: 19 October 2026. Corpus-surveillance recommendation, not a systematic review or a claim of complete coverage. No production files or screening decisions changed. No model API calls.

Including PCP does not require including the entire cannabinoid field. The defensible approach is an explicit, finite compound scope with a matching search strategy. Incidentally retrieved papers are insufficient evidence of comprehensive coverage for either drug.

Scientific interpretation

PCP fits the established dissociative category alongside ketamine. The NIH overview distinguishes classic serotonergic psychedelics, dissociatives, and selected other drugs. Our graph already spans these selected families rather than one receptor mechanism.

It would be inaccurate to assert that cannabis cannot produce psychedelic-like states. A 2018 controlled-dosing case reports marked hallucinatory effects after vaporized cannabis, with a subjective profile differing from comparison hallucinogens. A 2024 review discusses high-THC cannabis and psychedelic-like experiences, while emphasizing the need for further investigation. Neither establishes that all cannabis, CBD, endocannabinoid and cannabinoid-receptor research belongs in this project's core.

The earlier rationale that people use a substance for psychedelic purposes was too broad as a standalone eligibility rule. Excluding standalone cannabinoids is a deliberate project-scope decision; it should not be described as proof that cannabis never has psychedelic effects.

For example, the 2022 prospective survey of 321 people directly examines cannabis co-use during serotonergic psychedelic experiences. Reported associations with subjective effects make it relevant to the core-drug evidence. Its observational design does not establish causality. A generic CBD arthritis study, by contrast, would remain outside the core.

A concise methods statement could be: The graph covers a defined set of psychedelics, entactogens, dissociatives and selected atypical hallucinogens. Standalone cannabinoid research is outside the current scope. Cannabis may be represented in otherwise eligible research as a comparator, co-exposure, or treatment target. This does not imply comprehensive cannabinoid coverage. This is proposed text, not a published or implemented amendment.

A bounded way forward

  1. Keep the approved core and V5 starting set; do not launch another whole-corpus screening refinement.
  2. Use the existing historical compound-addition workflow for PCP in PubMed and OpenAlex, deduplicating against the current corpus.
  3. Inspect PCP alias precision before executing. The current generated plan contains bare PCP, which also means primary care and Pneumocystis pneumonia. Full-name and controlled-vocabulary branches should preserve drug coverage while acronym branches need context. This plan was inspected, not executed.
  4. Send genuinely new candidates through normal title/abstract Luna screening. For included papers, recover usable accessible full text and then extract through the existing workflow. There is no fulltext screening followup.
  5. Keep PCP coverage explicitly provisional until this retrieval reconciles. PCP additions can be processed as a separate increment rather than delaying unrelated core extraction.
  6. Consider a cannabinoid expansion only as a deliberate future module with a separately budgeted search and extraction plan. Do not promote the incidental cannabis subset as representative coverage.

The newly added Phencyclidine allowlist entry is already detected by the existing scope-delta planner: it schedules all-time identity recovery from 1800 in both providers. No new discovery architecture is needed. The two provider search definitions may expand into multiple pagination/partition requests.
