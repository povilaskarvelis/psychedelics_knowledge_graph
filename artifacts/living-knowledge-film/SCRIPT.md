# Living Knowledge — launch film script (v21)

1920×1080 (rendered 2x and downscaled) · 30 fps · 1:46 · on-screen type, no voiceover · original score at 120 bpm
(1 beat = 15 frames, 1 bar = 2 s)

Spine: in 1665 the first scientific journals appear, and for 360 years science has been recorded in papers. For
centuries papers trickle in; now the pile explodes. Findings, methods and context are buried in prose, so patterns
across studies remain implicit; reviews and meta-analyses document some of these patterns, but they take months
and quickly become outdated; finding every relevant study means sifting through thousands, and every reader starts from
scratch. It's time to rethink how scientific knowledge is stored, synthesized and accessed. AI can now read an entire
field into one living knowledge graph. We built the first one for psychedelic science (real product, live MCP session).
Now imagine this for all of science.

## Director's brief

- **One journey.** Act I is a single camera move to the right. The pile grows along it while the camera pulls back to
  take in its height; after 2026 the camera keeps going and one paper flies out of the pile. Each problem is the next
  station on the same line, and each one hands its papers to the next: the paper and its neighbours are bound into a
  review, the review's timeline fills with new studies, those studies join thousands of search results, and when a
  research team has screened some of them the screening is wiped and an AI agent starts again from zero.
- **Pages are pages.** Pages seen up close are drawn at full resolution, with titles of real length. The first paper
  (blue and gold bar chart) and the paper read up close (grey bars, so the highlights carry the colour) are one-offs
  that appear nowhere else in the pile. Figures sit in their own boxes and never touch the text; the highlighter marks
  sit on words.
- **Plain words.** Say things the way people say them: no unusual constructions ("science's unit of record"),
  nothing vague ("the paper is born"). Colour in a line means one thing: when findings, methods and context are named in
  their colours, nothing else in that line is gold.
- **Time to read.** Every line stays up long enough to read at a calm pace (roughly a second per three words once it has
  fully appeared), using the quiet moments around it rather than lengthening the film; nothing else crosses the type
  while it is up.
- **Type is composed, not subtitled.** Acts I–II keep a type column on the right and the picture on the left, with
  nothing crossing between them. The column carries the year as a heading during the growth, the search box during
  screening, and short hand-broken lines. Keywords take a highlighter mark in the same colour, at the same moment, as
  the highlight on the page. In Act III the product takes the whole frame and each line is a headline across the top.
  Turning points are set large and centred: the thesis, "AI can now read an entire field." on black, and the last two
  lines under the network of fields. Type waits for each camera move to land.
- **The product, compressed.** Act III shows psychedelicskg.com as motion graphics built from its real numbers rather
  than as screenshots. Explore drills down from broad patterns to a single study in a few clicks: the field → one
  compound → one compound–condition pair (75 studies, 30 RCTs) → the findings behind it → one finding (an RCT, read
  into findings, methods and context) → the paper it comes from. Analyze gives the whole field at a glance in three
  panels built from the site's Analyze data: publication history (the site's own chart: papers per year, 2000–2025,
  stacked as primary studies, reviews and meta-analyses; 178 in 2000, 1,806 in 2025), coverage (who or what each
  compound's primary studies were done in: people, animals, cells), and gaps (conditions × compounds; one click swaps
  primary studies for meta-analyses and the pairs with ten or more primary studies but no meta-analysis are outlined in
  gold). The graph describes patterns, coverage and
  gaps; it makes no claims about the strength of evidence. Then an agent's MCP session draws its answer.
- **No overlays.** No vignettes, gradients, flashes, halos, rings or chromatic aberration. The background stays a
  flat #080a0b. Bloom only lights the blade in Act II; the graph has none.
- **Precise words.** Papers report findings, methods and context. Name both reviews and meta-analyses. State problems
  directly, without "it isn't X, it's Y" framing. The ending describes potential ("imagine"), not a product claim.
- **A review is a different paper, still a paper.** A gold masthead ("Systematic review & meta-analysis"), a PRISMA
  flow and a forest plot ending in the pooled diamond, bound thicker with a gold spine. It greys as the years after it
  fill with new studies.
- **The graph is the hero.** Every cluster is a dandelion: a hub, sub-hubs on stems, and florets of leaves on straight
  spokes. One cluster sits at the heart so the graph is full, not a shell. Each topic has its own hue, and the hues flow
  across the field from teal through blue, violet, rose and coral to gold (the gold of "living" beside it). Crisp flat
  dots with a dark rim, threads in their clusters' colours, no glow. Extracted pieces arrive in the type colours and
  settle into their topic's colour as they join the graph. In the coalescence every page travels into the graph with
  what was read from it, the pieces unfold into their places and the threads grow as they land; nothing appears from
  nowhere, and moving pieces are motion-blurred, never trailed by copies. New papers stream in at one steady pace and
  come apart as they fly: each piece leaves its page at the page's speed, tumbles, takes its highlighter colour, rounds
  into a node and slows only as it lands, the title band last as the paper itself. Pages are motion-blurred like the
  nodes. Only things that mean something move: no sparks run along the threads.
  The living shot keeps the whole graph clear of the type and the frame edge. In Act IV every other field has its own
  hue.
- **One colour code for what a paper reports.** Gold = findings, rose = methods, teal = context (on pages, in type and
  in the pieces the blade extracts); pale grey squares = papers.
- **Real where it matters.** Act III uses captures of psychedelicskg.com and results from the live API.
- **Motion has mass.** Cameras, cursors and panels move on closed-form damped springs; act cuts are frame-matched.

Factual anchors: the first scientific journals (Journal des sçavans, Philosophical Transactions) appeared in 1665.
Several million articles are published per year. Systematic reviews commonly take over a year (Borah et al., 2017),
and many are out of date within a few years (Shojania et al., 2007). Act I numbers are illustrative. Act III numbers
come from the live site and API (release of 2026-09-17), captured 2026-09-28/29. Analyze: the publication history is the
site's Publication history panel (2000–2025; 2026 is still under way), checked on the live site 2026-10-30; coverage
(compound × population/model) and
the gaps matrix from the site's Evidence coverage view (Papers set to Primary studies and to Meta-analyses; opioid use disorder and cluster
headache have no meta-analyses in the release).
