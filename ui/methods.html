Methods

How the Psychedelics Knowledge Graph is assembled: what literature is searched, how papers are screened, how evidence is extracted, and how everything is maintained.

Why

We are entering the era of agentic science, where AI agents can support many steps of knowledge generation. This creates an opportunity to address long-standing inefficiencies in academic workflows: findings are scattered across papers, evidence is split across disciplinary silos, and reviews require substantial expert labor, become static snapshots once published, and are difficult to query or reuse. As a result, human researchers struggle to see relationships, trends, gaps, and the overall shape of a field, while AI agents cannot easily use the evidence directly.

Agentic tools make it possible to build a new type of living evidence system, where literature discovery, screening, evidence extraction, visualization, and updating are part of a continuous workflow. Findings can be converted into structured, provenance-rich records that remain linked to their source papers and evidence locators, so the evidence base can be searched, corrected, reused, and extended as the literature changes.

The Psychedelics Knowledge Graph applies this model to psychedelic research, where a fast-growing literature spans clinical, biological, brain, behavioral, and translational work that is often read separately. Screened papers are converted into structured evidence records that power an interactive graph and dashboard. Human researchers can move from field-level patterns and gaps to source studies visually, while agents and analytic tools can query the same provenance-rich evidence directly.

Pipeline Overview

The workflow is designed to keep literature discovery broad while making graph inclusion conservative. Searches cast a wide net across clinical, biological, brain, behavioral, subjective, treatment-context, and real-world evidence. Each paper is then interpreted according to what it actually contains and how much source text is available, so the graph shows findings that can be traced back to specific papers. Automated screening and structured extraction help process the literature at scale, while source links, evidence locations, and validation rules keep the results auditable.

Define the Evidence Scope

The project starts with explicit vocabularies for psychedelic compounds and evidence domains: targets, pathways and readouts, brain systems, cognitive and behavioral function, subjective experience, pharmacokinetics and exposure, intervention context, real-world use, clinical outcomes, functioning, and safety. These vocabularies define what the graph can represent and what the search needs to cover.

Find Papers

PubMed and OpenAlex searches combine broad domain queries, focused compound-topic queries, and supplementary direct-pair checks. Results are matched by DOI and merged into a single paper library. Metadata enrichment uses different sources for different needs: PubMed, PMC, OpenAlex, Crossref, and Semantic Scholar for bibliographic records and abstracts; PubMed for publication-type labels; and Unpaywall, OpenAlex, and PMC for open-access full-text or PDF links.

Screen and Classify Papers

Papers first pass an initial rules-based screen. Title and abstract screening then assesses relevance, identifies the evidence topics substantially discussed, and classifies the paper type. Primary studies, meta-analyses, other reviews, and non-primary context remain separate throughout the later workflow.

Extract Structured Evidence

Primary studies, meta-analyses, and other reviews use different extraction instructions and data structures so their evidence is not flattened into one type. The resulting findings retain compounds or exposures, related entities or outcomes, study context, reported direction, and source locations. When article text is available, PDFs or PMC text are converted into structured content that preserves sections, tables, figures, and references. Otherwise, extraction is limited to what the abstract reports.

Validate and Publish

Extracted findings are checked for structure, consistency, and source support, then compounds and related entities are matched to controlled names. The public overview shows relationships that can be represented clearly and meet its display rules. More specific or less easily normalized findings can remain searchable in paper details without becoming overview nodes.

Maintain the Living Graph

The graph is designed to evolve as the literature grows. New papers, corrected metadata, improved evidence extraction, and community feedback can all update the evidence base. Candidate releases are built and checked before publication. The active graph, bibliography, Methods counts, and public site are then updated together as one reviewed release.

Literature Search Strategy

The search is organized around evidence domains: targets, pathways and readouts, brain systems, cognitive and behavioral functions, subjective experience, pharmacokinetics and exposure, intervention delivery context, real-world use and public health, clinical outcomes and safety, and clinical studies that measure biological or behavioral endpoints.

PubMed was used for curated biomedical indexing, and OpenAlex was used for broader scholarly coverage across journals, books, and preprints. Searches use the same three-block structure: compound terms, domain-specific entity or outcome terms, and evidence-context terms. Terms inside each block are joined with OR; the blocks are joined with AND. Broad modules cover domain families, while focused modules target well-studied compound-topic combinations so that important papers are not captured only through broad queries.

Limits, updates, and reuse

This is a living map of the available evidence, not a complete or final systematic review and not clinical guidance. Coverage depends on the search, source indexing, record quality, and access to source text. Findings remain linked to their papers, and primary studies, meta-analyses, and other reviews are kept separate. The project does not currently assign a formal certainty grade or risk-of-bias judgment to every finding.

Quality checks

Findings are checked for the expected structure, source support, and consistent names. A finding can remain in paper details even when it cannot be shown safely in the overview graph.

Coverage limits

Search terms, database indexing, missing identifiers or abstracts, access restrictions, and article conversion all shape coverage. A missing graph node is not proof that no evidence exists.

Ongoing updates

New papers and corrections are processed at the paper, screening, or extraction stage. A reviewed release refreshes the graph, bibliography, Methods counts, and public site together.

Reuse and licensing

Project-created public data is released under CC0, and the code is licensed under Apache 2.0. Articles, abstracts, excerpts, and provider records remain subject to their original terms.
