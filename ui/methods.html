Methods

How the Psychedelics Knowledge Graph is assembled: what literature is searched, how papers are screened, how evidence is extracted, and how graph edges stay traceable.

Why

What should evidence synthesis look like in the age of agentic science? Traditional reviews remain essential, but static papers are slow to produce, hard to keep current, and difficult for computational systems to build on directly. Agentic tools make a different model possible: living evidence bases that can be updated as new studies appear, while preserving the long-standing scientific ideal of careful, transparent review.

For that model to be useful, the evidence base has to serve people and machines at the same time. Researchers, clinicians, and curious readers should be able to navigate the literature through clear interfaces; agents and analytic tools should be able to query structured, provenance-rich data and extend it responsibly.

The Psychedelics Knowledge Graph is an experiment in that kind of evidence infrastructure: open, documented, inspectable, and designed to improve through community feedback as the field changes.

Pipeline Overview

The pipeline follows a familiar evidence-synthesis flow: define the scope, search the literature, screen candidate records, retrieve full texts where possible, and extract evidence. The added step is to turn the extracted evidence into structured, provenance-rich records that can power a graph, dashboard views, gap analyses, and future agentic workflows. Each step leaves an auditable artifact so the evidence base can be inspected, corrected, and rebuilt as the literature changes.

Define the Evidence Scope

The project starts with explicit vocabularies for psychedelic compounds, mechanistic targets, and clinical indications. Those vocabularies define the two evidence lanes used throughout the site: compound-target and compound-indication relationships.

Discover Candidate Papers

PubMed and OpenAlex searches combine broad topic queries with direct compound-target and compound-indication searches. Results are matched by DOI, enriched with metadata from sources such as Crossref, Unpaywall, PubMed/PMC, OpenAlex, and Semantic Scholar, and merged into a paper library without duplicating records already present.

Screen and Retrieve

Candidate records pass through deterministic and LLM-assisted abstract screening. Relevant and uncertain papers continue to full-text retrieval when legally available; papers without usable full text remain labeled as abstract-only.

Extract Structured Evidence

Eligible papers are converted into structured evidence records with LLM-assisted extraction: compound, target or indication, study type, assay or outcome, result direction, evidence locator, access status, and source provenance.

Validate and Publish

Extracted records are checked against the schema and evidence policy before they enter the public graph. Uncertain or inconsistent records stay in review queues rather than being silently promoted. The same records also feed the PRISMA-style flow diagram, literature landscape, and gap view.

Update and Review

Search, screening, extraction, validation, and graph generation can be rerun when new papers or corrections appear. Community feedback is routed into the same maintenance process.

Literature Search Strategy

The search was designed around two relationship types: psychedelic compounds and mechanistic targets, and psychedelic compounds and clinical indications. The strategy uses grouped database searches for broad topic areas and direct pairwise searches for every compound-target and compound-indication combination in the vocabulary. Searches were run in PubMed for curated biomedical indexing and OpenAlex for broader scholarly coverage.

The dropdowns below show the actual term combinations used for the grouped database searches. The same search logic was used in OpenAlex and PubMed, with searches focused on titles and abstracts. Within each search, terms inside a block were joined with OR, and the compound, target or indication, and evidence blocks were joined with AND. Focused searches intentionally overlap with the broader searches; they use narrower compound-topic combinations for well-studied areas so those papers are not captured only through a broad query.

Limits, Updates, And Reuse

Coverage depends on the search vocabulary, source indexing, DOI metadata, and access to full text. LLMs help scale screening and extraction, but their outputs stay tied to reviewable records. Evidence labels and risk-of-bias notes are descriptive unless a formal certainty or risk-of-bias workflow is added.

Validation

Evidence records are checked against the expected data format and evidence-policy rules before they are included in the public graph.

Known Limits

Search terms, provider indexing, missing abstracts, access restrictions, and PDF conversion quality all shape coverage.

Living Evidence

New searches can be scheduled, new records can be screened, and the public graph can be rebuilt when accepted evidence changes.

Data Boundary

The public graph publishes identifiers, metadata, structured evidence, provenance, and project-written summaries. Source texts remain governed by article licenses and copyright.
