Methods

How the Psychedelics Knowledge Graph is assembled: what literature is searched, how papers are screened, how evidence is extracted, and how graph edges stay traceable.

Overview

This project is a living literature map for psychedelic compounds. It turns paper metadata and extracted evidence into a provenance-aware knowledge graph that can be inspected, rebuilt, and corrected as the literature changes.

The public interface shows a generated evidence graph. It summarizes two evidence lanes, keeps the search and screening record visible, and links graph edges back to the papers and evidence records that support them.

The sections below follow that path in order: search strategy, screening and extraction, paper flow, graph outputs, and limits.

01

Searchable Evidence

Literature discovery uses structured OpenAlex and PubMed searches for broad recall, plus direct compound-target and compound-indication searches for rare combinations.

02

Traceable Decisions

Search results, screening decisions, full-text access status, extraction status, and exclusions are kept as auditable records.

03

Structured Graph Data

Papers, evidence records, entities, provenance, and evidence-quality fields are stored as structured objects for the interface and other tools.

04

Updateable Workflow

Discovery, screening, extraction, validation, and graph generation can be rerun when new studies or corrections appear.

Literature Search Strategy

The search was designed around two relationship types: psychedelic compounds and mechanistic targets, and psychedelic compounds and clinical indications. The strategy uses grouped database searches for broad topic areas and direct pairwise searches for every compound-target and compound-indication combination in the vocabulary. Searches were run in OpenAlex and PubMed.

The dropdowns below show the actual term combinations used for the grouped database searches. The same search logic was used in OpenAlex and PubMed, with searches focused on titles and abstracts. Within each search, terms inside a block were joined with OR, and the compound, target or indication, and evidence blocks were joined with AND. Focused searches intentionally overlap with the broader searches; they use narrower compound-topic combinations for well-studied areas so those papers are not captured only through a broad query.

From Search to Extracted Evidence

Search results become graph evidence only after DOI matching, abstract screening, full-text checks, and structured extraction.

01

Literature Discovery

OpenAlex and PubMed results are matched by DOI before they enter the paper library, so papers already present are not added again.

02

Abstract Screening

A deterministic pre-screen removes records with no psychedelic signal. Retained abstracts are screened for relevance with a local Qwen3 14B LLM; relevant and uncertain papers continue.

03

Full-Text Access

Papers that pass screening are checked for legally available full text and converted when possible. Abstract-only papers remain labeled as abstract-only.

04

Evidence Extraction

Relevant papers are extracted into structured fields for the graph: compound, target or indication, study type, result direction, source location, and key study details.

Limits, Updates, And Reuse

Coverage depends on the search vocabulary, source indexing, DOI metadata, and access to full text. LLMs help scale screening and extraction, but their outputs stay tied to reviewable records. Evidence labels and risk-of-bias notes are descriptive unless a formal certainty or risk-of-bias workflow is added.

Validation

Evidence records are checked against the expected data format and evidence-policy rules before they are included in the public graph.

Known Limits

Search terms, provider indexing, missing abstracts, access restrictions, and PDF conversion quality all shape coverage.

Living Evidence

New searches can be scheduled, new records can be screened, and the public graph can be rebuilt when accepted evidence changes.

Data Boundary

The public graph publishes identifiers, metadata, structured evidence, provenance, and project-written summaries. Source texts remain governed by article licenses and copyright.
