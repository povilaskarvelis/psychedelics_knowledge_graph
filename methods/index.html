Methods

How Psychedelics Knowledge Graph is assembled: how literature is searched, how records are screened, how evidence is extracted from reports, and how everything is maintained.

Pipeline overview

The workflow is designed to keep literature discovery broad while making graph inclusion conservative. Searches cast a wide net across clinical, biological, brain, behavioral, subjective, treatment-context, and real-world evidence. Each publication is then interpreted according to what it actually contains and how much source text is available, so the graph shows findings that can be traced back to specific publications. Rules-based checks and LLM-based screening and evidence extraction allow the workflow to operate at scale, while source links, evidence locations within source publications, and validation rules keep the results auditable. The current workflow uses Gemini 3 Flash Preview for LLM-based title-and-abstract screening and evidence extraction. The pipeline is open source and available for inspection, reuse, and improvement. A detailed workflow guide documents its implementation.

Define the evidence scope

The project starts with explicit vocabularies for psychedelic compounds and research areas: targets, pathways and readouts, brain systems, cognitive and behavioral function, subjective experience, pharmacokinetics and exposure, intervention context, real-world use, clinical outcomes, functioning, and safety. These vocabularies define what the graph can represent and what the search needs to cover.

Find records

PubMed and OpenAlex searches combine broad searches across the main research areas with compound-identity searches that are not restricted to a particular topic. Newly added compounds are also searched across the historical literature. Results are matched by DOI and merged into a single set of records. Metadata enrichment uses PubMed, PMC, OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, and Unpaywall for bibliographic information, publication types, abstracts, and accessible full text.

Screen and classify records

Explicit rules first remove records that are clearly ineligible or irrelevant. An LLM then uses each title and abstract to determine whether the publication is within scope and identify its publication type and the research areas it addresses. These classifications determine which extraction instructions are applied.

Prepare source text

Reusable full-text XML is retrieved directly from Europe PMC or PubMed Central when available. Otherwise, accessible PDFs are retrieved and parsed with GROBID into TEI XML. Both routes preserve the article's sections, tables, figures, references, and source locations in a form that can be processed by the extraction workflow. The document identity is checked before use.

Extract evidence

LLM-based extraction uses instructions tailored to primary studies, meta-analyses, and other reviews, the relevant area of evidence, and whether full article text or only an abstract is available. The extracted findings include the compounds or exposures studied, related concepts or outcomes, study context, and locations in the source publication.

Validate and publish

Extracted findings are checked for the required fields and for support in the source publication. Accepted terminology is then standardized before the graph is assembled. Relationships that are unclear, unsupported, or cannot be represented accurately are left out.

Maintain the living graph

The graph is designed to evolve as the literature grows. Newly discovered records, selected publications, corrected metadata, improved evidence extraction, and research community feedback can all update the evidence base. Proposed updates are checked before publication. The graph, bibliography, Methods counts, and public site are then updated together.

Literature search strategy

The active search strategy uses PubMed and OpenAlex to cover the main research areas represented in the graph: targets, pathways and readouts, brain systems, cognitive and behavioral functions, subjective experience, pharmacokinetics and exposure, intervention context, real-world use and public health, clinical outcomes and safety, and clinical studies with biological or behavioral endpoints. PubMed searches combine text words with MeSH and Supplementary Concept terms, while OpenAlex searches titles and abstracts.

Two complementary types of search are used. Research-area searches combine psychedelic compound or class terms with broad vocabulary for each research area. Compound-identity searches use compound names and aliases without limiting results to a particular area, allowing relevant publications to be classified during screening. When a new compound is added to the project scope, its earlier literature is also searched so that the graph does not begin tracking it only from that point forward.

Together, these choices define the current scope and sensitivity of the search. The strategy aims to identify relevant literature across all research areas represented in the graph, but its coverage is limited by the selected compounds, evidence vocabularies, query structures, and databases. It is therefore maintained as an evolving part of the pipeline. The graph represents the cumulative results of all completed searches, and identified gaps can be addressed by revising vocabularies, adding searches for specific identified gaps, or extending the search to underrepresented areas. Newly retrieved publications can then be screened and incorporated into the graph. The research-area vocabularies used in the active strategy are shown below.

Screening and classification

Screening is organized as two successive stages. The rules-based stage handles decisions that can be made consistently from bibliographic metadata and text availability. It excludes records with no abstract; books, book chapters, theses, conference abstracts, and preprints; protocols, corrections, peer-review records, and citation-only records; and records with no recognized psychedelic compound or intervention term in the title, abstract, author keywords, or MeSH terms. Narrow rules also remove known false positives, such as LSD referring to lumpy skin disease. Each decision and exclusion reason is retained with the record.

The remaining records are screened with Gemini 3 Flash Preview. The decision is based primarily on the title and abstract, with publication labels, MeSH terms, and keywords used as supporting context. A publication is considered in scope when a psychedelic-related substance, intervention, exposure, or relevant research area is a central subject of the study or a major topic of the synthesis. Background examples, incidental mentions, comparison points, and individual items in broad lists are excluded. When relevance is plausible but the abstract is limited or ambiguous, the publication is retained for the next stage to reduce the chance of excluding relevant evidence.

The same LLM response identifies the publication type and every area of evidence the publication substantially addresses, based on its study question, measured outcomes, original findings, or main synthesis topics. These classifications determine whether the extraction procedure for primary studies, meta-analyses, or reviews is used. For primary studies, they also determine which research areas are extracted.

1. Rules-based screening Metadata and text rules

Records are excluded before LLM screening when any of the following applies:

  • No abstract is available.
  • The publication is a book, book chapter, thesis, preprint, or conference abstract.
  • The record is a protocol, correction, peer-review record, or citation-only record.
  • No recognized psychedelic compound or intervention term appears in the title, abstract, keywords, or MeSH terms.
  • The text matches a known false positive, such as LSD referring to lumpy skin disease.

Source-text preparation

For each eligible publication, the pipeline first looks for reusable full-text XML from Europe PMC or PubMed Central. This source is available directly in Journal Article Tag Suite (JATS) XML, a machine-readable representation of the article's sections, paragraphs, tables, figures, references, and identifiers. When an XML record contains more than one article or sub-article, the pipeline retains only the entry whose DOI matches the requested publication.

When reusable JATS XML is unavailable, the pipeline retrieves an accessible PDF and uses GROBID to parse it into Text Encoding Initiative (TEI) XML. GROBID reconstructs the article hierarchy from the PDF, including section headings and paragraphs, and identifies tables, figures, references, and their locations. The original XML, parsed sections, and source metadata are stored together. Before the text is used for evidence extraction, identifiers and title metadata are checked to confirm that the source corresponds to the intended publication. The source-text workflow documents retrieval, file validation, conversion, and provenance in more detail.

Evidence extraction

Evidence extraction begins only after the publication type and best available source text have been established. Primary studies are processed using instructions for the research areas identified during screening, with a focus on original empirical findings. Meta-analyses are processed as quantitative syntheses, preserving their synthesis-level questions and results. Other reviews are processed once at the paper level to identify the relationships they address in substantive detail. This keeps findings from primary studies, pooled results from meta-analyses, and relationships described in reviews distinct throughout the graph.

Gemini 3 Flash Preview receives instructions matched to the publication type and to whether full article text or only an abstract is available. Prepared article text is used when available; otherwise, an eligible publication can be processed with separate abstract-only instructions that limit the output to information stated in the abstract. For primary studies, the instructions are tailored to the relevant research areas and specify the fields to be returned. The model is asked to extract only evidence stated in the supplied source, preserve the publication's terminology, and provide a source location when available. Extracted findings are checked for the required fields before their terminology is standardized. Results that contain no extractable evidence or require human review are recorded but are not added to the graph automatically.

Standardization and graph inclusion

Before extracted findings are added to the graph, their terminology is standardized while the wording and context from the publication are preserved. Alternative names and spellings for the same compound are mapped to a standard compound name. Compound classes, combinations, exposure contexts, and treatment regimens remain distinct. Other extracted concepts are matched to consistent labels, aliases, or broader parent categories only when the mapping preserves their meaning. The repository provides the compound and entity registry and the controlled vocabularies for graph concepts.

Inclusion in the graph is deliberately conservative. A finding must identify a compound, exposure, or intervention and a concept or relationship that can be represented consistently without changing its meaning. Findings that cannot yet be standardized confidently are flagged for review rather than added to the graph. The same applies to findings that require human review or are supported only by background text. Valid information that is too specific for the visualization may remain searchable without being shown directly. The graph inclusion rules document how these decisions are applied.

The visualization applies one further inclusion rule: concepts drawn from primary research and reviews must be supported by at least two distinct publications. Findings supported by only one publication remain searchable but do not appear in the visualization. Concepts drawn from meta-analyses are exempt because each meta-analysis already represents a synthesis.

Validation, publication, and updates

Before publication, the standardized evidence is assembled into a draft version of the graph. Automated checks verify that screening decisions, extracted findings, standardized terminology, source publications, and decisions about inclusion in the graph are complete and consistent. The draft does not replace the public graph until it has passed these checks and been reviewed.

The graph, API data, bibliography, PRISMA counts, and Methods data are generated from the same reviewed evidence base so that the public views remain consistent. Later searches pass through the same workflow, while corrections can replace the extracted findings for selected publications without reprocessing unaffected literature. This allows new evidence, improved standardization, and research community feedback to be incorporated without rebuilding the project from the beginning. The publication workflow describes the checks applied before updates are published.

Limitations

The graph may not include every relevant report, and automated screening and extraction can miss or misinterpret information, especially when only an abstract is available because the full article cannot be accessed. Gemini 3 Flash Preview was selected to balance model capability with the cost of processing literature at scale; more capable models could improve screening or extraction quality, but would also increase processing costs. The graph is intended for research exploration, not as a complete systematic review or as clinical guidance.
