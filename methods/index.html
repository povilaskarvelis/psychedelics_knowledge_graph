Methods

How Psychedelics Knowledge Graph is assembled: how literature is searched, how records are screened, how evidence is extracted, and how everything is maintained.

Pipeline overview

The workflow is designed to keep literature discovery broad while making graph inclusion conservative. Searches cast a wide net across clinical, biological, brain, behavioral, subjective, treatment context, and real-world evidence. Each publication is then interpreted according to what it actually contains and how much source text is available, so the graph shows findings that can be traced back to specific publications. Rules-based checks and LLM-based screening and evidence extraction allow the workflow to operate at scale, while source links, evidence locations within source publications, and validation rules keep the results auditable. The current workflow uses Gemini 3 Flash for LLM-based title-and-abstract screening and evidence extraction. The pipeline is open source and available for inspection, reuse, and improvement. A detailed workflow guide documents its implementation.

Define the evidence scope

The project starts with explicit vocabularies for psychedelic compounds and research areas: targets, pathways and readouts, brain systems, cognitive and behavioral function, subjective experience, pharmacokinetics and exposure, intervention context, real-world use, clinical outcomes, functioning, and safety. These vocabularies define what the graph can represent and what the search needs to cover.

Find records

PubMed and OpenAlex searches combine broad searches across the main research areas with compound searches that are not restricted to a particular topic. Newly added compounds are also searched across the historical literature. Results are matched by DOI and merged into a single set of records. Metadata enrichment uses PubMed, PMC, OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, and Unpaywall for bibliographic information, publication types, abstracts, and accessible full text.

Screen and classify records

Explicit rules first remove records that are clearly ineligible or irrelevant. An LLM then uses each title and abstract to determine whether the publication is within scope and identify its publication type and the research areas it addresses. These classifications determine which extraction instructions are applied.

Prepare source text

Reusable full-text XML is retrieved directly from Europe PMC or PubMed Central when available. Otherwise, accessible PDFs are retrieved and parsed with GROBID into TEI XML. Both routes preserve the article's sections, tables, figures, references, and source locations in a form that can be processed by the extraction workflow. The document identity is checked before use.

Extract evidence

LLM-based extraction uses instructions tailored to primary studies, meta-analyses, and other reviews, the relevant area of evidence, and whether full article text or only an abstract is available. The extracted findings include the compounds or exposures studied, related concepts or outcomes, study context, and locations in the source publication.

Validate and publish

Extracted findings are checked for the required fields and for support in the source publication. Extracted compounds and concepts are then normalized to consistent graph entities before the graph is assembled. Relationships that are unclear, unsupported, or cannot be represented accurately are left out.

Maintain the living graph

The graph is designed to evolve as the literature grows. Newly discovered records, selected publications, corrected metadata, improved evidence extraction, and research community feedback can all update the evidence base. Proposed updates are checked before publication. The graph, bibliography, Methods counts, and public site are then updated together.

Literature search strategy

The search strategy is organized around a defined set of psychedelic compounds and the research areas represented in the graph. The compound vocabulary includes compound names, selected chemical, botanical, and common aliases that identify them with sufficient specificity, and broader psychedelic class terms. Terms for each research area were selected to cover its major concepts, methods, measures, and outcomes, with PubMed subject headings added where available. The compound vocabulary is combined with these research area terms to retrieve literature addressing each area. Each compound and its aliases are also searched without research area terms to identify potentially relevant publications whose wording falls outside those predefined vocabularies. When a compound is added to the project scope, searches also cover its earlier literature rather than only publications appearing after it was added.

The research area searches cover molecular targets and pathways, brain systems, cognition and behavior, subjective experience, pharmacokinetics, intervention context, real-world use and public health, clinical outcomes and safety, and biological or behavioral endpoints in clinical studies. PubMed and OpenAlex are searched because their overlapping coverage and different indexing systems provide complementary routes to relevant records. PubMed queries use text words together with MeSH and Supplementary Concept terms, while OpenAlex queries search titles and abstracts. Results are matched by DOI and merged. The graph represents the cumulative results of all completed searches. Coverage depends on the compounds, aliases, research area vocabulary, query structures, and databases used, so the strategy can be revised as gaps are identified and newly retrieved publications can be screened and incorporated. Screening and classification

Screening is organized as two successive stages. The rules-based stage handles decisions that can be made consistently from bibliographic metadata and text availability. It excludes records with no abstract; books, book chapters, theses, conference abstracts, and preprints; protocols, corrections, peer-review records, and citation-only records; and records with no recognized psychedelic compound or intervention term in the title, abstract, author keywords, or MeSH terms. The remaining records are screened with Gemini 3 Flash. The decision is based primarily on the title and abstract, with publication labels, MeSH terms, and keywords used as supporting context. A publication is considered in scope when a psychedelic-related substance, intervention, exposure, or relevant research area is a central subject of the study or a major topic of the synthesis. Background examples, incidental mentions, comparison points, and individual items in broad lists are excluded. When relevance is plausible but the abstract is limited or ambiguous, the publication is retained for the next stage to reduce the chance of excluding relevant evidence. LLM-based screening also determines the publication type and the research areas the paper addresses. These classifications determine the evidence extraction strategy used in the next stage.

1. Rules-based screening Metadata and text rules

Records are excluded before LLM screening when any of the following applies:

  • No abstract is available.
  • The publication is a book, book chapter, thesis, preprint, or conference abstract.
  • The record is a protocol, correction, peer-review record, or citation-only record.
  • No recognized psychedelic compound or intervention term appears in the title, abstract, keywords, or MeSH terms.
  • The text matches a known false positive, such as LSD referring to lumpy skin disease.

Source text preparation

For each eligible publication, the pipeline first looks for reusable full-text XML from Europe PMC or PubMed Central. This source is available directly in Journal Article Tag Suite (JATS) XML, a machine-readable representation of the article's sections, paragraphs, tables, figures, references, and identifiers. When an XML record contains more than one article or sub-article, the pipeline retains only the entry whose DOI matches the requested publication. When reusable JATS XML is unavailable, the pipeline retrieves an accessible PDF and uses GROBID to parse it into Text Encoding Initiative (TEI) XML. GROBID reconstructs the article hierarchy from the PDF, including section headings and paragraphs, and identifies tables, figures, references, and their locations. The original XML, parsed sections, and source metadata are stored together. Before the text is used for evidence extraction, identifiers and title metadata are checked to confirm that the source corresponds to the intended publication. The source text workflow documents retrieval, file validation, conversion, and provenance in more detail.

Evidence extraction

To extract relevant findings from a paper, the pipeline supplies three inputs to an LLM: the available source text, an extraction prompt describing what evidence to identify, and an output schema defining the fields and structure in which the findings must be returned. The prompt and output schema are selected based on the publication type and, for primary studies, the research areas identified during screening. Separate prompt variants are used for full article text and abstracts, with abstract extraction limited to information stated in the abstract. Primary study prompts focus on original empirical findings. Meta-analysis prompts preserve the questions and pooled results of the quantitative synthesis, while review prompts identify relationships addressed in substantive detail. Across all publication types, the LLM is instructed to extract only evidence stated in the supplied source, preserve the publication's terminology, and provide a source location when available. Extracted findings are checked for the required fields before their compounds and concepts are normalized. Results that contain no extractable evidence or require human review are recorded but are not added to the graph automatically.

Entity normalization and graph inclusion

Before extracted findings are added to the graph, compound names and other extracted concepts are normalized to consistent graph entities while the wording and context from the publication are preserved. Alternative names and spellings for the same compound are mapped to the same compound entity. Compound classes, combinations, exposure contexts, and treatment regimens remain distinct. Other extracted concepts are matched directly to existing graph entities or through recognized aliases. A broader parent entity is used only when the mapping preserves the original meaning. The repository provides the compound and entity registry and the controlled vocabularies for graph concepts. Inclusion in the graph is deliberately conservative. A finding must identify a compound, exposure, or intervention and a concept or relationship that can be represented consistently without changing its meaning. Findings whose compounds or concepts cannot yet be normalized confidently are flagged for review rather than added to the graph. The same applies to findings that require human review or are supported only by background text. Valid information that is too specific for the visualization may remain searchable without being shown directly. The graph inclusion rules document how these decisions are applied.

Limitations

The graph may not include every relevant report, and automated screening and extraction can miss or misinterpret information. Evidence extraction is more limited when only an abstract is available. Full-text retrieval depends on the availability and accuracy of article links and open-access metadata supplied by external providers. These records are not always complete or consistent, so the workflow may fail to retrieve some articles that are in fact openly accessible. Gemini 3 Flash was selected to balance model capability with the cost of processing literature at scale; more capable models could improve screening or extraction quality, but would also increase processing costs. The graph is intended for research exploration, not as a complete systematic review or as clinical guidance.
